# Memo of Understanding — Conversation Checklist

*Prepared as if by an outside consultant meeting this project for the first time. This is a checklist for a founder conversation and a starting brief for a lawyer — it is not legal advice, and nothing here substitutes for counsel licensed in your state. The goal: leave the first working meeting with a one-page written understanding both people would be comfortable re-reading in two years, whether the project succeeded, died, or got weird.*

**The situation as published:** trypassalong.com is Danielle's brand and idea — a founder-led, pre-launch concept ("A more human way to find mental health care") with her name and story on it. A prospective technical partner (Shane) is considering joining. That asymmetry — her brand + idea, his build capacity — is the thing every item below exists to make explicit.

---

## 1. Spirit and scope (write this paragraph first)

- [ ] One sentence each, written independently then compared: *what do you each think this project is?* (Misalignment here invalidates everything below.)
- [ ] What this MOU covers: the exploration/prototype period only? Through launch? Define an end date or trigger (e.g., "we revisit in writing at launch or in 6 months, whichever is first").
- [ ] Explicit statement that this is a non-binding understanding pending a real agreement — **except** the IP and confidentiality items, which you likely want binding from day one (lawyer question).

## 2. What each person brings (inventory before ownership)

- [ ] Danielle's contributions, listed: the Pass Along name/brand/domain, the concept, founder story and public identity, clinical credential and judgment, audience/distribution, community relationships.
- [ ] Shane's contributions, listed: engineering and AI architecture capability, time commitment (specify hours/week honestly), infrastructure costs fronted, **any pre-existing prototype or exploratory work either party brings to the table** — inventoried by date, so "what existed before we agreed" is never a memory contest later.
- [ ] Cash: is either party putting money in? Even $50/mo of hosting should be written down (reimbursable? contribution?).

## 3. Ownership of the idea, brand, and code — pick a structure

Present these as options; the wrong move is defaulting into ambiguity:

| Option | Shape | Fits when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Her company, his contract** | Danielle forms the entity; Shane contributes as contractor/advisor with defined comp (cash, revenue share, or equity grant w/ vesting) | She sees this as *her* company; he wants bounded involvement | Work-for-hire language must be explicit or IP defaults get murky |
| **B. Co-founded newco** | New entity; both assign relevant IP into it; equity split negotiated (see §4) | Both intend to build this as partners | Brand/domain must be formally assigned in, not "lent" |
| **C. Build-then-decide** | Short dated agreement: prototype period, mutual confidentiality, IP parked (each keeps their own; neither ships) until a go/no-go date forces Option A or B | Today's reality — pre-discovery, pre-commitment | Must include what happens to jointly-touched work if the answer is "no-go" |
| **D. Open-source the engine** | Code released under permissive license; the *company* is brand + corpus + operations | Maximizes trust story; kills code-as-moat (which is arguably not the moat anyway) | Needs both parties' genuine comfort, not ideology |

- [ ] Who owns the brand/domain **today** (confirm registration details) and does that change under the chosen option?
- [ ] Who owns the **corpus** (user submissions)? This outlives everything else and is the real asset — name it now.
- [ ] If pre-existing prototype work exists: is it licensed to the venture, assigned, or discarded? (An explicit sentence either way.)

## 4. Equity / compensation (only if Option A or B)

- [ ] Split rationale written down, not just numbers (idea + brand + clinical license + distribution vs. build + ongoing engineering — there are frameworks like Slicing Pie for dynamic splits if a fixed number feels premature).
- [ ] **Vesting for both parties** (standard: 4-year, 1-year cliff) — protects each from the other walking early with a full share.
- [ ] What happens to equity if either becomes inactive? Define "inactive."
- [ ] Any salary/fee expectations before revenue? (Usually none; say so.)

## 5. Decision rights and roles

- [ ] Who decides product direction? (The published site is founder-voice; if the answer is "Danielle," write it down and mean it.)
- [ ] Who decides clinical/safety/moderation policy? (This one should be the licensed clinician, unambiguously.)
- [ ] Who decides technical architecture and vendor choices?
- [ ] Spending threshold above which both must agree.
- [ ] Deadlock: what happens when you disagree? (Named tiebreak topic-by-topic beats a fake 50/50.)

## 6. The trust-and-safety obligations (unusual for an MOU — necessary here)

Because this is a mental-health-adjacent consumer product, put non-negotiables in writing early — they protect both of you and the users:

- [ ] Crisis-response commitment: any public-facing flow includes crisis routing (988) before launch. *(Consultant note: the current live site has none — this is the single most urgent gap on the published property, fixable in an afternoon.)*
- [ ] Privacy floor: no ad-tech/trackers on intake or search flows, ever; user text is never sold or shared; a real privacy policy exists before any collection (FTC precedent in this exact category: BetterHelp, $7.8M).
- [ ] The "never paid placement" principle: if it's a brand commitment, make it a written one — it constrains the business model and both parties should sign up for that constraint knowingly.
- [ ] Who is the named responsible party for moderation decisions and data-rights requests?

## 7. Liability, entity, and insurance

- [ ] No public launch under an informal partnership — entity first (LLC at minimum). Date or trigger for formation.
- [ ] Until then: what is each party's exposure and what activities are off-limits (e.g., collecting real user stories)?
- [ ] Insurance shopping list for launch: general liability, cyber, E&O; media liability worth asking about.
- [ ] Which state's laws, given user-privacy statutes vary (WA MHMDA-style laws have private rights of action)?

## 8. Confidentiality and each other's stuff

- [ ] Mutual confidentiality covering the concept, any user data ever collected, and each other's private information.
- [ ] Danielle's audience/community relationships are hers; Shane's other projects/tooling are his — neither is captured by the venture unless explicitly contributed.

## 9. The unwind (decide while you like each other)

- [ ] If the project dies: brand/domain returns to/stays with ___; code goes to ___; **any collected user submissions are handled how** (deleted? archived? never transferred/sold — write that down)?
- [ ] If one person exits: the other's rights to continue, under what name, owing what.
- [ ] If someone wants to buy it / fund it: right of first discussion between partners before third-party talks.

## 10. Cadence and revisit

- [ ] Working cadence (weekly call? async?), and a scheduled MOU-revisit date.
- [ ] Success criteria for the next 90 days that both endorse (see the Cold-Start Playbook) — an MOU without a shared near-term definition of progress is a friendship-preservation device, not a plan.

---

*Sequence suggestion: items 1–3 and 6 are the first conversation. Items 4–5 need a second sitting. Items 7–9 are the lawyer visit. Do not launch anything public before 6 and 7 exist.*
